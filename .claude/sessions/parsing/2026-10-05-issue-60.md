# Session: Issue #60 Remote duty holders (CLOSED)

## Problem

About 2,782 passive and thing-subject duties hub-wide don't name their holder, so the definitive prompt (`drrp-v1.0`) labels them as holder unknown. Often the holder is named in another provision that applies the duty. For example, in the Workplace Regulations, `UK_uksi_1992_3004:reg.6(1)` ("Effective and suitable provision shall be made to ensure that every enclosed workplace is ventilated…") gets its holder from `reg.4(1)` ("Every employer shall ensure that every workplace … complies with any requirement of these Regulations"). The prompt never sees `reg.4(1)`: it follows only citations going out, and `reg.6` cites nothing. If the training labels are made before this is fixed, the SLM learns "no holder" for these duties, so this must be settled before labelling (build first, run once).

## Rule (agreed 2026-10-05)

Written into `docs/architecture/DRRP-CLASSIFICATION.md`: a new special case, "Holder named in an applying provision (#60)", plus a worked example and a conformance row (not implemented). The layer 1 passive-duty note and the "Holder unknown" row now point to it.
- The applying provision must **name** the party and put it under a duty to comply with, or ensure compliance with, requirements whose scope covers this provision. The scope can be explicit (a range or Schedule) or instrument-wide ("these Regulations", "this Part").
- That party is `active`, holds the Obligation and is marked `inferred: true` (`extraction_method = inferred`). Every applying provision whose scope covers the duty contributes its holder.
- A scope clause that names no party gives no holder. There's never a "dominant actor" default; #60's original proposal 2 is dropped.
- Worked case: Workplace Regs 1992 `reg.6(1)` is applied by `reg.4(1)` (employer), `reg.4(2)` (person in control; `reg.4(4)` carves out the self-employed) and `reg.4(5)` (deemed occupier of a factory). All three are instrument-wide ("any requirement of these Regulations").

## Resolver (2026-10-05)

`applying_index(texts, parts)` → per law, the applying provisions; `applying(section_id, parts, index)` → the ones whose scope covers a provision (max 4; never its own stem or items). Code: `scripts/drrp_prompt.py`.
- **Matches:** `shall|must` … `comply with [the requirements of] SCOPE`, or `ensure that the requirements of SCOPE are complied with`.
- **SCOPE:** these Regulations / this Order / this Act (whole law, not Schedules), this Part (`part` column), Part N, Schedule N, regulation/section/article numbers, ranges and sub-refs. Scopes in other instruments ("of the Construction and Use Regulations", "Article 21 of RAMS") are rejected.
- **Excluded:** compliance mentioned without a duty to comply, i.e. offences, failure to comply, "in order to / necessary to comply", enabling, presumption, notices, evidence, "whether", "does not", Member States.
- **List items:** when the applying provision's holders sit in its own items ("It shall be the duty of every— (a) employer …"), the items are appended.
- **Workplace Regs:** `reg.6(1)` resolves to `reg.4(1)` (employer) and `reg.4(2)` (person in control). `reg.4(5)` (deemed occupier) is also an applying provision and covers it too.

**Result:** 101 applying provisions in 50 laws. Largest gains:
- DPA 2018 Part 3 (157; `s.56(1)` controller);
- Building Regs 2010 (120);
- CDM 2015 (61; `reg.16` contractor and domestic client);
- Workplace Regs (55);
- Merchant Shipping H&S 1997 (45);
- NI CDM 2016 (26).

**Holder-unknown breakdown** (17,641 substantive, live Obligation provisions with no active actor, by current tiers):

| Bucket | n | Fixed by |

So #60's applying-provision rule is a small, precise gain. Most "holder unknown" goes away when the definitive prompt labels each provision, which is what the training labels do anyway.

**Rulings (Jason, 2026-10-05), formerly open:**
1. **Supervisory duties: both are holders**, the supervisor and the persons it must make comply ("that's what the law actually requires of PCs"). Was: "The principal contractor must take all reasonable steps to ensure that … contractors and any other person … comply with the duties under these Regulations" (Building Regs `reg.11M(2)(c)`/`11N(2)(c)`). Proposal: when the applying provision has a party ensure that OTHER PERSONS comply, it isn't an applying provision. The supervisor holds its own supervisory duty there, and the persons' duties are labelled where created. "Ensure that a THING complies" (workplace, premises) stays an applying provision.
2. **Wider wording: include both.** Was: Product regimes say "ensure that the equipment has been designed and manufactured in accordance with the essential requirements" (e.g. EMC Directive Art. 6 "The equipment shall meet the essential requirements…"). Weighing-machine-style prohibitions say "No person shall use … unless it complies with these Regulations" (currently excluded by the `unless` filter). Add both forms?

## Rulings applied and prompt v1.1 (2026-10-05)

- **Spec:** the applying-provision row lists four forms: comply / ensure a thing complies; supervisory (both holders); essential requirements; prohibition ("no person shall … unless it complies with").
- **Resolver additions:**
  - the "unless" exclusion is gone;
  - paragraph ranges within the same provision ("paragraphs (3) to (6) of this regulation");
  - essential-requirements applying provisions cover every provision in the law that mentions the essential requirements;
  - a match must end in the provision's own text, so list items don't inherit their stem's match.
- **Result:** 206 applying provisions in 93 laws; 701 of 17,641 holder-unknown duties in 51 laws.
- **Prompt `drrp-v1.1-2026-10-05`:**
  - a new rule "Holder named in an applying provision", plus an APPLYING PROVISIONS context block (max 4, 1,200 characters each);
  - the passive-duty rule now points to the stem, referenced and applying rules;
  - `inferred` now covers applying holders;
  - it never turns a detail or no-relation provision into a relation.
- **Smoke test (Gemini 3.8 Flash, 8 calls, nothing written):** all as ruled.
  - Workplace `reg.6(1)`: employer, person in control and occupier;
  - CDM `reg.31(5)`: contractor and client;
  - Building Regs `reg.44F(3)(b)`: principal contractor and contractor (supervisory);
  - DPA `s.91(1)`: controller (as `OTHER:`, a dictionary gap);
  - EMC Directive Art. 6: manufacturer;
  - Workplace `reg.4(1)`: names its own holder, so the applying provisions are ignored;
  - two negatives (a holder only in the parent Act; minutes of the Authority): relation `no`, no holder guessed.
